Part A · Chapter 16

Straight line graphs

1 The Cartesian plane

Two number lines cross at the origin (0 ; 0). A point is written as an ordered pair (x ; y) — across first, then up or down.

QuadrantxyExample
I · top right++(3 ; 2)
II · top left−+(−3 ; 2)
III · bottom left−−(−3 ; −2)
IV · bottom right+−(3 ; −2)

Points on the x-axis have y = 0; points on the y-axis have x = 0.

2 The equation y = mx + c

  • m = the gradient — how steep the line is and which way it slopes.
  • c = the y-intercept — where the line cuts the y-axis, the point (0 ; c).
Always rewrite into y = mx + c form first.
2y = 6x − 8 → divide by 2 → y = 3x − 4, so m = 3 and c = −4.
3x + y = 5 → y = −3x + 5, so m = −3 and c = 5.

3 Gradient

m = rise / run = (change in y) / (change in x) m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
Worked example

Gradient of the line through A(1 ; 2) and B(5 ; 10):

m = (10 − 2) / (5 − 1) = 8 / 4 = 2

GradientThe line
m > 0Rises from left to right  /
m < 0Falls from left to right  \
m = 0Horizontal line, y = c
undefinedVertical line, x = a (you would divide by 0)
  • The bigger the size of m, the steeper the line — y = 4x is steeper than y = ½x.
  • Parallel lines have equal gradients: m₁ = m₂.

5 Three ways to draw a line

Table of values — safest, works every time

For y = 2x − 1:

x−1012
y−3−113

Plot the points, join them with a ruled line and label the line with its equation.

Dual-intercept method — fastest when c ≠ 0

For y = 2x − 4: let x = 0 → y = −4, so (0 ; −4). Let y = 0 → 0 = 2x − 4 → x = 2, so (2 ; 0). Rule the line through the two intercepts.

Gradient and intercept

Plot (0 ; c), then step out the gradient — for m = 3/2, up 3 and right 2 — and plot a second point.

6 Finding the equation from a graph

  1. Read off the y-intercept — that is c.
  2. Pick two clear points and calculate m.
  3. Substitute both into y = mx + c.
Worked example

A line cuts the y-axis at −1 and passes through (2 ; 5).

c = −1
m = (5 − (−1)) / (2 − 0) = 6/2 = 3
∴ y = 3x − 1

Given two points only, find m first, then substitute one point to solve for c. Through (1 ; 4) and (3 ; 10): m = 6/2 = 3, then 4 = 3(1) + c → c = 1 → y = 3x + 1.

7 Does a point lie on the line?

Substitute it in — if the left side equals the right side, it lies on the line.

  • (3 ; 5) on y = 2x − 1?  2(3) − 1 = 5 ✓ yes
  • (4 ; 6) on y = 2x − 1?  2(4) − 1 = 7 ≠ 6 ✗ no

8 Point of intersection

Two lines cross where x and y are the same for both. Read it off the graph, or solve the equations together:

y = x + 1 and y = −x + 5 x + 1 = −x + 5 → 2x = 4 → x = 2, y = 3 intersection (2 ; 3)

9 Reading real-life graphs

  • A straight graph means a constant rate of change.
  • The gradient is the rate — rand per hour, km/h, cost per item.
  • The y-intercept is the fixed starting value — a call-out fee, a deposit, a head start.
  • Discrete data (learners, cars): plot separate points, do not join them. Continuous data (time, mass): join with a solid line.
  • A horizontal section means no change — stationary, or the cost stays the same.
Worked example

A plumber charges C = 250h + 400, where h is hours worked.

  • 400 — the fixed call-out fee (y-intercept)
  • 250 — the cost per hour (gradient)
  • 3 hours: C = 250(3) + 400 = R1 150

Data handling

1 The data-handling cycle

Pose a question → collect → organise (tally / frequency table) → represent (graph) → analyse (mean, median, mode, spread) → interpret and report.

2 Types of data

TypeMeaningExample
Qualitative (categorical)Descriptions, wordsEye colour, favourite sport
QuantitativeNumbersHeight, test marks
— DiscreteCounted, exact valuesNumber of siblings, goals
— ContinuousMeasured, any value in a rangeMass, time, length
PrimaryYou collected it yourselfYour own class survey
SecondaryCollected by someone elseNewspaper or Stats SA figures

3 Sampling and bias

  • Population — every member of the group being studied. Sample — the smaller part actually surveyed, which must be random and representative.
  • Bias is anything that makes the result unfair: a sample that is too small or one-sided (only asking your friends), leading questions ("Don't you agree that…?"), or options that overlap or are ambiguous.
  • A good questionnaire question is short, clear, unbiased, and its answer options do not overlap.

Grouped data is used when there are many different values. Intervals must be equal and must not overlap: 0–9, 10–19, 20–29 or 0 ≤ x < 10, 10 ≤ x < 20.

Always give a key. Every original value is kept and the data comes out in order, which makes the median easy to find.

Every graph needs a title, labelled axes with units, a sensible scale starting at 0, and a key if there is more than one data set.

6 Measures of central tendency

MeasureHow to find itWatch out
MeanSum of values ÷ number of valuesPulled badly by outliers
MedianOrder the data first, then take the middle valueBest when there are outliers
ModeThe value that appears most oftenThere can be none, one, or several

Median position with n values is (n + 1) ÷ 2. With 7 values it is the 4th; with 8 values, average the 4th and 5th.

Worked example

Data: 4, 7, 7, 9, 13

mean = (4+7+7+9+13)/5 = 40/5 = 8
median = 7  (3rd of 5 values)
mode = 7

Working backwards — a favourite exam question

The mean of 5 numbers is 12. Four of them are 10, 14, 9 and 15. Find the fifth.

total = 12 × 5 = 60
10 + 14 + 9 + 15 = 48
fifth number = 60 − 48 = 12

From a frequency table: mean = Σ(value × frequency) ÷ Σfrequency. For grouped data you can only give the modal class and the interval containing the median.

7 Measures of spread

  • Range = maximum − minimum. A big range means widely spread data.
  • An outlier sits far from the rest. It drags the mean but barely moves the median.
  • Quartiles cut the ordered data into four: Q₁ is the median of the lower half, Q₂ is the median, Q₃ is the median of the upper half.
  • Interquartile range: IQR = Q₃ − Q₁ — the spread of the middle 50%.
  • Five-number summary: minimum, Q₁, median, Q₃, maximum → draw a box-and-whisker, as below.
Worked example

Data: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 15, 18  (9 values, already in order)

min = 2, max = 18, range = 16
median = 8  (5th value)
lower half 2, 4, 5, 7 → Q₁ = (4+5)/2 = 4,5
upper half 10, 12, 15, 18 → Q₃ = (12+15)/2 = 13,5
IQR = 13,5 − 4,5 = 9

8 Which measure is best?

  • An extreme outlier — such as one very high salary — use the median.
  • Categorical data, like which shoe size to stock — use the mode.
  • Evenly spread data with no outliers — use the mean, since it uses every value.

9 Misleading graphs and interpretation

Be ready to say why a graph misleads:

  • The vertical axis does not start at 0, or is broken — small differences look huge.
  • Uneven scale intervals.
  • 3-D effects or pictures of different sizes that distort the comparison.
  • Missing title, labels or key.
  • A conclusion drawn from a sample that is too small or biased.
When a question says “comment on” or “compare”, mention the centre (mean or median), the spread (range or IQR) and anything unusual (outliers, a trend) — then say what it means in the context of the question.

Practice

Work each one out on paper first, then reveal the answer.

01

Write 4y = 8x − 12 in the form y = mx + c and state m and c.

Show answer
y = 2x − 3  →  m = 2, c = −3
02

Calculate the gradient of the line through P(−2 ; 3) and Q(4 ; 15).

Show answer
m = (15 − 3) / (4 − (−2)) = 12/6 = 2
03

Determine the x- and y-intercepts of y = −3x + 9.

Show answer
y-intercept (0 ; 9)  ·  x-intercept: 0 = −3x + 9 → x = 3, so (3 ; 0)
04

Find the equation of the line with gradient −2 that passes through (1 ; 5).

Show answer
5 = −2(1) + c → c = 7 → y = −2x + 7
05

Does the point (−1 ; 4) lie on the line y = −3x + 1?

Show answer
−3(−1) + 1 = 4 ✓  Yes, it lies on the line.
06

Give the equation of a line parallel to y = 5x − 2 that cuts the y-axis at 7.

Show answer
Parallel → same gradient → y = 5x + 7
07

For 3, 8, 8, 9, 12, 14, 20 find the mean, median, mode and range.

Show answer
mean = 74/7 ≈ 10,57  ·  median = 9  ·  mode = 8  ·  range = 20 − 3 = 17
08

In a class of 45 learners, 15 chose soccer. What angle represents soccer on a pie chart?

Show answer
15/45 × 360° = 120°
09

The mean of 6 numbers is 9,5. Five of them are 8, 11, 7, 12 and 10. Find the sixth.

Show answer
total = 9,5 × 6 = 57  ·  8+11+7+12+10 = 48  ·  sixth number = 57 − 48 = 9
10

For 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, 14, 17, 20 give the five-number summary and the IQR.

Show answer
min 5 · Q₁ = (6+8)/2 = 7 · median = (10+11)/2 = 10,5 · Q₃ = (14+17)/2 = 15,5 · max 20
IQR = 15,5 − 7 = 8,5
11

A graph's vertical axis starts at 80 instead of 0. Explain why this could be misleading.

Show answer
The differences between the bars are exaggerated, so one bar looks far bigger than another even though the actual values are close together.
12

Explain the difference between discrete and continuous data, with an example of each.

Show answer
Discrete data is counted in whole units — the number of learners in a class. Continuous data is measured and can take any value in a range — height in centimetres.
Before you write

Exam tips

  • Ruler and pencil for every graph. Label the axes, the intercepts and the line itself with its equation.
  • Show all your working — method marks are awarded even when the final answer is wrong.
  • Rewrite into y = mx + c before reading off a gradient. Never read m straight off 2y = 6x − 8.
  • Order the data before finding a median or quartiles. This is the most commonly lost mark in the whole data section.
  • Check units and rounding — one decimal place, rand, degrees.
  • “Explain” and “comment on” need a sentence in the context of the question, not just a number.
  • 50 marks in 60 minutes ≈ one minute a mark. Attempt every question, even partly.
